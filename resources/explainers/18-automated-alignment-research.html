Claude as Alignment Researcher

Anthropic Research · Aug 28, 2026 · read the source
Can an automated research loop find alignment fixes that hold up outside the room they were found in?

Given a search → propose → train → test loop and a second model watching for cheating, Claude closed 26%–96% of the safety gap across 10 alignment-failure categories — and on the one category with full numbers published, beat 28 human researchers by roughly 4x.

01The problem it exists to solve

Models are being built faster than humans can hand-check them for deception, sycophancy, jailbreak susceptibility, reward hacking, and similar failures. Manual alignment research doesn't scale with that pace, and even when a mitigation is proposed, "did it actually work" has never had a clean, comparable score.

This work tries to answer both at once: automate the researcher, and use the automation's own output — % of a defined safety gap closed — as the score that was missing.

02How it works

One loop per failure category, run separately for each of the 10. A second model reads every proposed method before it runs — not after, as an audit — and can veto it.

The idea most worth stealing: the anti-gaming rules are stated as explicit, checkable constraints — no capability degradation, no direct distillation of the teacher's own alignment into the student — not left to the loop's judgment. A monitor enforces them by reading intent before execution, mirroring this pool's enforcement-boundary idea that a rule which can be violated silently wants to be checked mechanically, not requested in a prompt.

05The one thing to remember

The safety net here isn't a smarter prompt — it's a second model reading the plan before the first model is allowed to act on it, with explicit, named rules it's checking against (no capability loss, no direct distillation). That structure is the reusable idea; the specific percentages are one study's result.

06What we take from it

Take these

  • Monitor-before-execution, not audit-after. The gate reads intent, not output.
  • Anti-gaming rules named explicitly, not left implicit in "be safe."
  • The monitor's own blind spot stated openly — detection depends on the misbehavior surfacing in reasoning, and may not hold for future models.

Leave these

  • The 15,000x efficiency figure as a general claim — it prices one found solution, not the search.
  • Treating this as directly buildable here. Model-training-layer alignment research is a level below this repo's harness-engineering scope.
